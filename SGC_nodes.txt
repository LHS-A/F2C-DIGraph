Normal: no clinically significant retinal abnormalities are observed and retinal structures appear within normal limits

Diabetic Retinopathy: a retinal microvascular disease caused by diabetes mellitus characterized by vascular damage and retinal ischemia

Age-Related Macular Degeneration: a degenerative retinal disease affecting the macula and causing progressive central vision loss

Retinal Vein Occlusion: retinal vascular occlusion caused by obstruction of retinal venous circulation resulting in retinal ischemia and hemorrhage

Central Serous Chorioretinopathy: a retinal disorder characterized by serous detachment of the neurosensory retina due to choroidal dysfunction

Retinal Vasculature: the network of retinal arteries, veins, and capillaries supplying blood to the retina

Retina: the light-sensitive neural tissue lining the inner surface of the eye

Macula: the central region of the retina responsible for detailed central vision

Retinal Pigment Epithelium: a pigmented cellular layer supporting photoreceptor function and retinal metabolism

Choroid: the vascular layer between the retina and sclera supplying oxygen and nutrients to the outer retina

Retinal Veins: venous vessels responsible for draining blood from the retina

Retinal Capillary Network: the microvascular capillary system supplying retinal tissue

microaneurysms: small red dots on the retina, usually the earliest sign of diabetic retinopathy

intraretinal microvascular abnormalities: abnormal tiny blood vessels growing within the retina due to retinal ischemia

venous beading: irregular dilation and constriction of retinal veins associated with retinal ischemia

retinal neovascularization: abnormal new blood vessels growing on the retinal surface due to ischemia

hard exudates: small, yellowish-white lipid deposits on the retina

cotton wool spots: fluffy white patches on the retina, indicating localized nerve fiber layer damage

retinal hemorrhage: bleeding within the retinal tissue

ischemic retina: retinal regions demonstrating extensive capillary dropout and poor perfusion

macular ischemia: reduced macular perfusion associated with abnormalities of the foveal avascular zone

foveal avascular zone: the capillary-free region located at the center of the macula responsible for sharp vision

drusen: small yellowish deposits beneath the retina

geographic atrophy: large areas of retinal pigment epithelium and photoreceptor loss causing blind spots

window defect: increased fluorescence due to retinal pigment epithelium loss allowing greater visualization of choroidal fluorescence

choroidal neovascularization: abnormal new blood vessels growing beneath the retina

choroidal filling: appearance of fluorescein within the choroidal circulation during angiography

retinal distortion: straight lines may appear wavy or curved due to retinal surface distortion

central scotoma: a central area of reduced or absent vision affecting the visual field

blood vessel dilation: retinal veins appear widened and tortuous

vascular tortuosity: abnormal twisting and curvature of retinal blood vessels demonstrated during angiography

capillary nonperfusion: areas lacking retinal capillary blood flow appearing as hypofluorescent regions

arteriovenous transit: passage of fluorescein dye from retinal arteries to retinal veins during angiography

dot and blot hemorrhages: small to medium-sized red retinal hemorrhages

blocked fluorescence: reduced fluorescence caused by blood, pigment, or other material obscuring underlying fluorescence

fluorescein leakage: progressive spread of fluorescein dye indicating breakdown of the blood-retinal barrier

pooling: accumulation of fluorescein dye within a fluid-filled anatomical space

subretinal fluid: accumulation of fluid between the neurosensory retina and the retinal pigment epithelium

serous retinal detachment: elevation of the neurosensory retina caused by subretinal fluid accumulation

pigment epithelial detachment: localized separation of the retinal pigment epithelium from the underlying Bruch membrane
